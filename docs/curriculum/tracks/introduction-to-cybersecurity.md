# Introduction to Cybersecurity

*Syllabus v0.1 (draft for practitioner review) · Licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0*

> **Protect your accounts, devices, money and data, and understand how the world of cybersecurity works.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **For** | Everyone: people new to tech, people at work, and anyone thinking about a security career |
| **Prerequisites** | None. IT Fundamentals helps, but isn't needed. |
| **Estimated effort** | ~35 hours across 10 modules (about 65 lessons) plus a capstone (≈ 2–3 months at 4 hours a week) |
| **Tools** | Our own simulated labs in the browser: an inbox, a password tester, a browser, a phone and a network. Nothing risky ever touches the learner's real accounts or devices. |
| **Primary alignment** | [NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework): its six functions (govern, identify, protect, detect, respond and recover), applied to people and small organisations |
| **Also aligned to** | [CISA Secure Our World](https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world) · UK NCSC [Cyber Aware](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/cyberaware) guidance · NIST SP 800-50 Rev. 1 (security and privacy learning) · [NICE Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/applied-cybersecurity/nice/nice-framework-resource-center) (NIST SP 800-181r1) for the roles · CompTIA Tech+ domain 6.0 · SFIA 9 level 1 (Follow): SCTY |
| **Credential** | PractiCode Learn Certificate: Introduction to Cybersecurity (Open Badges 3.0) |

## By the end, you can

1. Explain what cybersecurity protects (confidentiality, integrity and availability) and judge a risk by its likelihood and impact.
2. Secure your accounts with passphrases, a password manager, two-factor sign-in and passkeys.
3. Recognise phishing, social engineering and common scams, wherever they arrive, and report them.
4. Keep devices and software safe, and explain what each kind of malware does.
5. Browse and connect safely, and explain what HTTPS and VPNs do and don't protect.
6. Manage your privacy and digital footprint, and use your rights under data protection law.
7. Respond calmly and in the right order when an account, a device or your data is compromised.
8. Follow good security practice at work, including with AI tools.
9. Describe the main roles in cybersecurity, the "hats" and teams, and the law and ethics that separate security work from crime.

## Modules

Every module ends with a check and a hands-on project, and includes a *Myth or fact* step on the misconceptions people commonly hold about its topic.

| # | Module | Key outcomes | NIST CSF 2.0 | Project |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Why security matters to you** · *Free* | Explain what cybersecurity is; use confidentiality, integrity and availability to describe harm; tell threats, vulnerabilities and risk apart; describe who attacks and why; rate a risk | Govern, Identify | Your personal risk map: what you'd lose, and how likely it is |
| 2 | **Passwords and signing in** | Explain how passwords are stolen; make strong passphrases; use a password manager; set up two-factor sign-in and passkeys; plan account recovery | Protect | Secure three practice accounts from scratch |
| 3 | **Phishing and social engineering** | Spot phishing by email, text, phone and QR code; recognise pressure tricks; read a link before tapping it; report a phish | Detect | Sort a simulated inbox: real, phishing or suspicious |
| 4 | **Scams and fraud** | Recognise shopping, payment-alert, SIM-swap, investment, romance, job and tech-support scams; spot AI voice and video fakes; report fraud | Detect, Respond | A scam warning card for someone you care about |
| 5 | **Devices and software** | Explain what updates fix; describe viruses, worms, trojans, ransomware and spyware; review app permissions; secure and find a lost phone | Protect | A security check-up of a practice phone |
| 6 | **Safe browsing and networks** | Explain what the padlock does and doesn't prove; use public Wi-Fi safely; explain what a VPN does; secure a home router; manage extensions and cookies | Protect | Harden a practice home network |
| 7 | **Privacy and your data** | Map your digital footprint; tune privacy settings; explain tracking; use your rights under data protection law (GDPR as the example, and your own country's law); help children stay safe | Protect, Govern | A privacy clean-up plan for your own accounts |
| 8 | **When things go wrong** | Back up against ransomware with the 3-2-1 rule; follow a first-hour checklist after a hack or breach; recover accounts; know when and where to report | Respond, Recover | An incident plan for a family or small shop |
| 9 | **Security at work** | Apply least privilege; follow policies that protect people; report incidents early; use personal devices and AI tools without leaking data; recognise insider risk | Govern, Protect | A one-page security guide for new staff |
| 10 | **The world of cybersecurity** | Describe white, black and grey hats, and red, blue and purple teams; explain the law and ethics of hacking and bug bounties; describe the main roles with the NICE Framework; correct myths about the field | Govern | A profile of one security role, from real job descriptions |
| ★ | **Capstone** | Assess and improve the security of a real small organisation or household, with permission | All six | A security assessment and action plan |

## Lessons

Each lesson takes 8–12 minutes and follows the [lesson format](../lesson-format.md).

1. **Why security matters to you** (6): What cybersecurity protects · Confidentiality, integrity and availability · Threats, vulnerabilities and risk · Who attacks, and why · How attacks reach ordinary people · Thinking in risk: likelihood and impact
2. **Passwords and signing in** (7): How passwords get stolen · Passphrases beat complexity · Password managers · Two-factor sign-in · Passkeys · Security questions and account recovery · Your password plan
3. **Phishing and social engineering** (7): What phishing is · Reading a link before you tap · Lookalike addresses and domains · Text, call and QR code phishing · Pressure tricks: urgency, fear and authority · Pretexting and impersonation · Reporting a phish
4. **Scams and fraud** (7): How scams work · Shopping and payment-alert scams · SIM swaps · Investment and crypto scams · Romance and job scams · Tech-support scams · AI voice and video fakes
5. **Devices and software** (7): Updates close doors · Malware: viruses, worms and trojans · Ransomware and spyware · Why antivirus isn't enough · App permissions · Downloading safely · Lost or stolen phones
6. **Safe browsing and networks** (6): What the padlock means · Public Wi-Fi · What a VPN does and doesn't do · Your home router · Browser extensions · Cookies and tracking
7. **Privacy and your data** (6): Your digital footprint · Social media settings · Tracking and data brokers · Your data rights · Oversharing and doxxing · Keeping children safe online
8. **When things go wrong** (6): The 3-2-1 backup rule · Ransomware: prepare, don't pay · Your first hour after a hack · Recovering an account · When your data is in a breach · Reporting and getting help
9. **Security at work** (6): Why attackers target workplaces · Least privilege · Policies that protect people · Personal devices at work · AI tools and data leaks · Insider risk and reporting
10. **The world of cybersecurity** (7): Hackers: white, black and grey hats · Red, blue and purple teams · The law and ethics of hacking · Bug bounties and responsible disclosure · Roles in cybersecurity · Myths about working in security · Where to go next

## Labs

Every lab is a simulation, so learners practise on fake accounts and fake messages, never real ones:

- **CIA triad** (Module 1): one incident, seen through confidentiality, integrity and availability.
- **Risk matrix** (Module 1): rate likelihood and impact and watch a risk move.
- **Password theft** (Module 2): the five ways passwords really get stolen, and what stops each.
- **Crack clock** (Module 2): how long a password lasts after a breach, carelessly or properly stored.
- **Reuse dominoes** (Module 2): one breach and five accounts, by password habit.
- **Vault** (Module 2): a password manager generating, filling, and refusing to fill on a fake site.
- **Second factors** (Module 2): text codes, apps, push and security keys against four real attacks.
- **Passkey handshake** (Module 2): two keys, a challenge, and why a fake site gets nothing.
- **Recovery check** (Module 2): ways back into an account, rated.
- **Inbox** (Modules 3–4): real and phishing messages to inspect, with headers and links to hover.
- **Practice phone and browser** (Modules 5–6): permissions, updates, padlocks and extensions.
- **Home network** (Module 6): router settings to harden.

## Assessment

In-lesson tasks with instant checks, module mastery checks (80% pass mark), rubric-marked projects, and a capstone.

## Device notes

Every module works on a phone. Learners never test anything on systems they don't own or have permission to test: Module 10 explains why, and the law behind it.
