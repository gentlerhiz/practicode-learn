Three things we believe about learning

We didn't start with a business plan. We started with a classroom, and these are the things it taught us.

  1. i.

    You learn by doing, not watching.

    Researchers at Carnegie Mellon found that hands-on activities taught about six times more than video or reading. So in every lesson, you're the one at the keyboard.

  2. ii.

    Your data bundle deserves respect.

    A lesson weighs under 150 KB, less than one second of HD video. Save a whole module on Wi-Fi and keep learning on the bus, at the shop, or when the light goes off.

  3. iii.

    Proof beats promises.

    Every skill maps to a framework employers already use, like SFIA and the MDN Curriculum. When your certificate says you can do something, it can show how.

What a lesson feels like

Ten to fifteen minutes, five small moves. Teachers across the UK use the same method, called PRIMM.

  1. Predict

    Guess what the code will do. Being wrong here is useful.

  2. Run

    See what actually happens. Were you right?

  3. Investigate

    Pull it apart with diagrams you control, one tap at a time.

  4. Modify

    Change one thing and watch what happens.

  5. Make

    Build your own version from a blank page.

Things people usually ask

Is it really free?+

Yes. Module 1 of every track is free for good, no card needed. If you want the rest, Pro starts with a 7-day free trial.

Why are there no videos?+

Because people learn more by doing than by watching, and video eats data. Things still move: the diagrams animate one step at a time, whenever you tap.

Can I learn on my phone?+

Every lesson works on a phone. For the bigger projects a laptop helps, and each track tells you upfront what you'll need.

Where does the design come from?+

The patterns are inspired by adire, the hand-dyed indigo cloth of Yorubaland. It felt right for something made in Ibadan and built by hand.
